\title{\textbf{Completeness of Relational Operations in Language Models}\\[3pt]
\large Results on a 500-Question Benchmark}
\author{Daryl Alfaro}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We report results for five language models on a 500-question benchmark that asks for \emph{every} item
satisfying a condition, over records whose exact answers are obtained by executing SQL. On the \nctx{}
questions whose records are in the prompt, mean recall is \recallmin--\recallmax, but only \exactmin--\exactmax\%
of answers are exactly the correct set; for four of the five models nearly all errors are omitted items. The
chance of a complete answer falls both as the number of correct items grows and as the table grows (pooled odds
ratios of \orpooledanswer{} and \orpooledtable{} per doubling, each adjusted for the other and for input format).
Alternative prompts did not improve completeness, and models that stayed complete used far more reasoning tokens
on larger answer sets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Process}
\paragraph{Benchmark.} The benchmark has 500 questions over \ntables{} synthetic tables (\nrecords{} records)
about fictional entities, so answers cannot come from memory. Each question is generated with the SQL query that
defines it, and its gold answer is that query's result in SQLite. The 400 \emph{in-context} questions put all
records in the prompt; 60 \emph{memory-only} questions filter fixed lists (U.S.\ states, chemical elements,
presidents' surnames); 40 \emph{retrieval} questions supply the top-$k$ BM25 results. For in-context enumeration,
exactly $g\in\{5,10,20,50,100\}$ of $n\in\{50,200,500,1000\}$ rows match. Answer size and table size vary
largely independently, but answers of 50 or more items never occur in 50-row tables (correlation of the log sizes
\sizecorr), so trends are estimated with both sizes and the input format in one model. Records are shown as
Markdown, CSV, JSON Lines or English sentences, rotating across questions. Table~\ref{tab:composition} lists the
slices.

\paragraph{Models.} DeepSeek V4 Pro and V4 Flash (OpenCode Go API), Claude Haiku 5.5 (Claude Code CLI
\claudecli, no tools) and MiMo-V2.6 Pro and Flash (OpenRouter), each with its default reasoning, temperature~0
where supported, and a 32{,}768-token output limit. Answers are returned as JSON.

\paragraph{Metrics.} For gold set $G$ and predicted set $P$: recall $|P\cap G|/|G|$, precision $|P\cap G|/|P|$,
and \textbf{exact-set accuracy} $\mathbb{1}[P=G]$, the primary measure. Each error is classified as
\emph{missing}, \emph{near-miss} (a garbled copy of a missing item), \emph{wrong} (in the data but not
qualifying), \emph{made-up} (not in the data) or \emph{duplicate}. An answer \emph{omits} if it misses a correct
item and \emph{commits} if it adds an incorrect one. Answers that were truncated at the output limit, could not be
parsed, or were refused by a safety filter are \emph{invalid}: they count as incorrect for exact-set accuracy but
are excluded from error-type counts, so a cut-off answer is not mistaken for omitted items.

\paragraph{Runs and statistics.} \textbf{E1}: all 500 questions, direct prompt. \textbf{E2}: a stratified
100-question subset with a ``be exhaustive'' prompt and a ``check every record first'' prompt. \textbf{E3}: the
subset again with the direct prompt. Intervals are 95\% (Wilson for proportions, question-level bootstrap for
means); model and prompt comparisons use paired question-level differences; trends with answer size use a
logistic regression on $\log_2$ of the number of correct items.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Overall completeness}
Table~\ref{tab:main} shows the \nctx{} in-context set-valued questions. Recall and precision are close to 1 for
every model, but exact-set accuracy ranges from \exactmax\% to \exactmin\%. The best model, \bestmodel, is
statistically tied with \tiedwithbest; the other \nsigbelow{} are significantly below it, by \deltamin{} to
\deltamax{} percentage points.

Errors are almost all omissions (Figure~\ref{fig:errors}; counts in Table~\ref{tab:errors}). Four models together

usually miss only a few items (median \medianmissed; \smallmissshare\% miss at most a tenth of the answer), which is
why recall stays high. MiMo-V2.6 Flash is the exception: it returned \flashmadeup{} made-up items,
\flashworstmade{} of them on a single \flashworstgold-item question. Box~1 shows one example of each. Across all
models, \ninvalid{} of the \neone{} E1 answers were invalid, including \nrefused{} refused by a safety filter
(Table~\ref{tab:invalid}).

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Completeness, answer size and table size}
On in-context enumeration (Figure~\ref{fig:size}, Table~\ref{tab:trend}), every model is complete on at least
\smallmin\% of questions with up to 10 correct items. With 50 or more, Claude Haiku 5.5 falls to \largeHaiku\%,
MiMo-V2.6 Pro to \largeMiMoPro\% and MiMo-V2.6 Flash to \largeMiMoFlash\%. Because large answer sets occur only in
larger tables, Table~\ref{tab:trend} estimates both effects together, adjusted for input format. Pooled across
models, each doubling of the answer set multiplies the odds of a complete answer by \orpooledanswer{}
(95\% CI \orpooledanswerci), and each doubling of the table by \orpooledtable{} (\orpooledtableci): both
factors matter on their own. Per model, the answer-size effect is significant for Claude Haiku 5.5 and MiMo-V2.6
Flash, and the table-size effect for Claude Haiku 5.5 and both MiMo models. The DeepSeek models fail too rarely
(\failDSPro{} and \failDSFlash{} of \nenum{} questions) for either effect to be estimated.
\takeaway{larger answers and larger tables each make a complete answer less likely.}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{By operation and input format}
Every operation except set operations produced incomplete answers from at least three models
(Table~\ref{tab:operation}). Enumeration, which has the largest answer sets, is hardest; set operations are
solved almost perfectly. Input format matters for some models (Table~\ref{tab:format}): MiMo-V2.6 Flash is
complete on \fmtMiMoFlashNl\% of questions given as English sentences against \fmtMiMoFlashJson\% as JSON Lines, and
Claude Haiku 5.5 on \fmtHaikuMd\% of Markdown and \fmtHaikuNl\% of sentence inputs against \fmtHaikuJson\% for JSON
Lines. The DeepSeek models are unaffected.
\takeaway{the effect is not specific to one operation; format effects are smaller and model-specific.}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Complexity of selection criteria}
Adding WHERE conditions did not make answers less complete (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}). MiMo-V2.6 Flash, the
model with the most failures, was exact on \condMiMoFlashone\%, \condMiMoFlashtwo\% and \condMiMoFlashthree\% of
selection questions with one, two and three conditions, and Claude Haiku 5.5 on \condHaikuone\%,
\condHaikutwo\% and \condHaikuthree\%. By construction, though, questions with more conditions match fewer rows
(median answer size \condsizeone, \condsizetwo{} and \condsizethree{} items), so condition count is confounded
with answer size here, and the 60 selection questions are too few to separate the two.
\takeaway{more conditions alone did not reduce completeness at this scale; a crossed design (conditions $\times$
answer size) is needed to test it properly.}

\subsection{Consistency across runs}
Two independent samples of the subset agreed on \agreemin--\agreemax\% of verdicts (Table~\ref{tab:repeats}). We also re-ran
13 failures, chosen by hand to cover models and operations, three times each (Table~\ref{tab:cases}). Some
recurred every time: Claude Haiku 5.5 was incomplete on the same memory-only question (U.S.\ states containing
``in'') in all three re-runs, omitting Maine every time, and DeepSeek V4 Pro and MiMo-V2.6 Flash were incomplete on the same 50-item list in every re-run, though not always missing the
same names (Box~1). In the two runs where MiMo-V2.6 Pro failed on that list, it omitted the same three employees.
Others, including Claude Haiku 5.5's misses on selection, JOIN, projection and COUNT questions, never recurred.
\takeaway{some failures are systematic, but many are run-to-run variation, so completeness is best reported as a
rate over many questions.}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Reasoning effort}
Median reasoning length rose with answer size for the models that stayed complete
(Figure~\ref{fig:reasoning}): from \rsmallDSPro{} to \rlargeDSPro{} tokens for DeepSeek V4 Pro and from
\rsmallHaiku{} to \rlargeHaiku{} for Claude Haiku 5.5. MiMo-V2.6 Flash often answered after one sentence of
reasoning; its \flashshallown{} answers with under 100 reasoning tokens were complete \flashshallowexact\% of the
time, against \flashdeepexact\% for the rest.
\takeaway{completeness on large answer sets coincides with much longer reasoning; this is an association, not a
controlled result.}

\FloatBarrier
\section{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
\item Mean recall of \recallmin--\recallmax{} corresponds to exact-set accuracy of only \exactmin--\exactmax\%;
\omitmin--\omitmax\% of in-context answers omit at least one correct item.
\item Errors are omissions, not additions, for four of five models; one model fabricates items on long lists.
\item Completeness falls as answer sets grow and, independently, as tables grow (pooled odds ratios
\orpooledanswer{} and \orpooledtable{} per doubling).
\item Prompt variants do not help; complete models reason far longer on large answer sets.
\item The DeepSeek models are complete on \exactDSFlash\% of in-context questions, so at this difficulty the
benchmark leaves little headroom for the strongest models.
\item Caveats: synthetic data from one seed; one sample per question in E1 (\flipmin--\flipmax\% of verdicts change on a second
sample); the answer-size trend rests on 100 enumeration questions per model; the retrieval slice measures the
retriever; failure cases were chosen by hand; \etwonote{} A stopped Claude Haiku 4.5 run (90 questions) is
excluded.
\end{itemize}

\url{https://github.com/daryl-888/llm-relational-completeness}.\\
Statistics follow E.~Miller, ``Adding Error Bars to Evals'' (2024), \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640}.
